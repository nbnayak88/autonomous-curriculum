# Business Gameboard Model --- Course, Webinar, Bonuses & Marketing Assets

**Project owner:** Siddharth\
**Core topic:** Business as a game\
**Audience:** Entrepreneurs, solopreneurs, coaches, consultants, experts, course creators, and community builders\
**Core idea:** Turn a business's customer journey into a game-like experience where customers play, progress, complete missions, earn recognition, and keep coming back.

---

## 1. Core Positioning

### Business Gameboard Model

Help entrepreneurs make their business itself into a game for their customers.

**Core reframe:** A funnel gets customers in. A gameboard keeps customers moving.

The model treats the customer as the **player**, the offer as the **game promise**, the customer journey as **levels**, actions as **missions and challenges**, recognition and progress as **rewards**, and customer wins as fuel for **retention, referrals, testimonials, and revenue**.

**Useful positioning line:** “Turn your business into a game customers love to play.”

**Additional hooks:**
- “Your business is not boring. Your customer journey is.”
- “Stop building funnels. Start building gameboards.”
- “Customers don't want more content. They want visible progress.”
- “A funnel gets the sale. A gameboard gets the comeback.”
- “If your community is silent, don't post more. Create missions.”

5. **Reward Engine** — How will progress, effort, and achievement be recognised?
6. **Growth Loop** — How do customer wins support retention, referrals, testimonials, and revenue?

## 3. Three-Day Course Journey

### Starter → Pro → Star

#### Stage 1: Starter
- **Indicative timeline:** 7–14 days
- **Suggested effort:** 30–45 minutes per day
- **Outcome:** Create the first draft of a Business Gameboard.
- **Skills:** Customer clarity, promise design, journey mapping, simplification.
- **Traits:** Curious, open-minded, willing to learn, action-oriented, comfortable with imperfect progress.
- **Mindset:** “I don't need to build the perfect game. I just need to build my first version.”

#### Stage 2: Pro
- **Indicative timeline:** 30–90 days
- **Suggested effort:** 45–60 minutes per day
- **Outcome:** Launch and test the gameboard with real customers.
- **Skills:** Mission design, reward design, community building, feedback systems, implementation tracking.
- **Traits:** Consistent, experimental, customer-focused, feedback-friendly, action-oriented.
- **Mindset:** “I don't just teach or sell. I create a journey that moves people forward.”

#### Stage 3: Star
- **Indicative timeline:** 6–12 months
- **Suggested effort:** 60–90 minutes per day
- **Outcome:** Build a scalable gamified business ecosystem.
- **Skills:** Growth-loop design, leadership, data thinking, experience design, scalable systems.
- **Traits:** Visionary, disciplined, strategic, community-driven, result-focused.
- **Mindset:** “My customer's progress is my business growth engine.”

**Journey summary:** Starter builds the board. Pro runs the game. Star grows the movement.

## 8. Community Code of Honor

### Six values and one rule for each
1. **Respect** — Treat every member's ideas, time, and experience with consideration.
2. **Ownership** — Take responsibility for your actions, commitments, and results.
3. **Curiosity** — Ask questions before making assumptions.
4. **Action** — Turn each key insight into a practical experiment.
5. **Honesty** — Share real progress and setbacks without exaggeration.
6. **Generosity** — Help others learn without expecting an immediate return.

### Community guidelines: do's and don'ts
- **Do:** Share progress, experiments, and lessons learned. **Don't:** Claim results you cannot substantiate.
- **Do:** Give specific, constructive feedback. **Don't:** Shame, insult, or belittle members.
- **Do:** Ask for help with a clear context and question. **Don't:** Spam promotions or unsolicited pitches.
- **Do:** Respect confidentiality. **Don't:** Share another member's private information or work without permission.
- **Do:** Celebrate effort and meaningful wins. **Don't:** Compare people's journeys as if everyone had identical starting points.
- **Do:** Keep discussions relevant and inclusive. **Don't:** Present opinions as guaranteed business outcomes.

### Three-step accountability partner system
1. **Commit:** Each partner states one specific goal and the next action with a deadline.
2. **Check in:** Partners meet briefly at an agreed cadence to report completed actions and obstacles.
3. **Reflect and reset:** Celebrate evidence of progress, learn from misses without blame, and set the next commitment.

## 11. Day 2 — Skills and Core Concepts

### Ten useful skills
1. Customer research — understand motivations, obstacles, and desired outcomes.
2. Outcome design — define a clear, measurable customer win.
3. Journey mapping — organise the customer experience into logical stages.
4. Instructional design — make learning and actions easy to understand.
5. Behavioral design — reduce friction and encourage meaningful action.
6. Storytelling — make the journey emotionally engaging and memorable.
7. Community facilitation — build belonging and peer participation.
8. Feedback and analytics — measure engagement and identify drop-offs.
9. Experimentation — test changes and learn from real behaviour.
10. Systems thinking — connect engagement, retention, referrals, and revenue.

### Six principles to accelerate progress
1. **Start Small** — A simple working version produces feedback faster than an elaborate plan.
2. **Design for the Player** — Customer needs should determine the game, not the entrepreneur's preferences alone.
3. **Make Progress Visible** — Clear milestones help players recognise movement.
4. **Reward Meaningful Action** — Reinforce actions that contribute to the customer's desired outcome.
5. **Close the Feedback Loop** — Use customer behaviour and feedback to improve the experience.
6. **Improve Through Experiments** — Test one change at a time and learn from the evidence.
